## Summary
Serial 220 of FBI file 62-HQ-83894 consists of an unsolicited March 19, 1950 letter (with Spanish original and English translation by Mrs. Sophia Saliba) from Miguel Angel Garcia Macias of Veracruz, Mexico, addressed to the "President of the Commission of Scientific Investigation of the United States of North America" in New York and stamped received by the FBI New York office on April 7, 1950 (p. 2, p. 5, p. 9). Garcia Macias, who styles himself a "Pianist Composer Discoverer and Ideographic Inventor," asserts that the United States already possesses "stratospheric aerostats" — i.e., flying saucers — powered by atomic force, and submits his own ideographic theories for the Commission's consideration (p. 2). He lists prior inventions he claims were stolen and patented by others, including a graduated dropper ("Gote-Graduns"), automatic shovels for dump trucks, optical examination rooms, a "Gradual Centimetric Music of the Future," and rail/road anti-collision devices ("Ferrotactometer") (pp. 2–3). His central proposal combines "Global Stability" (of a balloon) with "Semi-Global Stability" (of a parachute) into a "Super-Stability" body of conic-global form, propelled by stratospheric skyrockets using "Nuclear-Atomic force" at speeds said to exceed light or sound (pp. 3–4). The packet also includes translated newspaper captions and drawings about German Horacio Robles Jr., an engineering student in Durango, Mexico, said to be the first to photograph a flying saucer at 8,000–9,000 feet, described as a "double truncated cone, joined at its bases" moving by jet-like propulsion (p. 5, p. 13).
