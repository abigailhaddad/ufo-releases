## Summary
Section 4 (serials 131–185) compiles late-1947 and early-1948 field reports and citizen correspondence on flying discs (p. 1). Portland police including Chief LEON V. JENKINS and patrolmen ROBERT D. ADAIR and J. R. CALDWELL reported a bright silvery object over the city on September 11, 1947, with conflicting estimates of altitude, speed, and shape (pp. 2, 4). Pilot JACK PECK and co-pilot VINCE DALY described a fuselage-less "flying wing" near Bethel, Alaska in July 1947 while landing a DC-3 (pp. 5–6). Civilian letters speculate that discs caused forest fires or were foreign incendiary weapons, and Marion Beuscher forwarded an anonymous threat note signed "Government / Rainbows City / Antarctica" (pp. 15, 22, 27).

## Key facts
- Covers serials 131–185, declassified under the FBI Automatic Declassification Guide of May 24, 2007 (p. 1).
- Portland Police logged the first District 18 sighting at 5:21 PM on September 11, 1947 (p. 2).
- Officer ADAIR estimated the object at 5,000 feet and 150 mph, believing it sunlight on a metal airplane (p. 2).
- Chief JENKINS described a silver object at ~10,000 feet that became egg-shaped and vanished in 30 seconds (p. 4).
- Pilot JACK PECK reported a "flying wing" the size of a C-54 without fuselage going ~300 mph toward Nome (p. 5).
- Anchorage SAC vouched that PECK and DALY were "very reliable" and "definitely observed something" (p. 6).
- Joseph W. Omikine asked Hoover whether the FBI suppressed a newsreel of Russians examining a crashed saucer; Hoover cited confidentiality (pp. 18–20).
- Mary C. Foster reported Red Cross official Joe Lippincott told a dentist the saucers were "actually from Spain" (p. 21).
- Beuscher forwarded an anonymous Cincinnati-postmarked letter signed "Government Rainbows City Antarctica" warning her to "not mention anymore" (p. 22).
- The threat tied to W. C. Hefferlin's Amazing Stories letter referencing "Rainbow City" and "The Ancient Three" (pp. 26–27).
- A Covington, Ohio writer theorized the discs were foreign self-propelled test bombs ranging U.S. cities, possibly aimed near Fort Knox (pp. 29–30).
