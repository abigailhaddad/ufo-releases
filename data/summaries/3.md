## Summary
Section 3 of FBI file 62-HQ-83894 (serials 101–130) gathers FBI and Fourth Air Force correspondence from August–September 1947 on early "flying disc" sightings (p. 1). It includes prospector F. M. Johnson's letter describing oval ~30-foot discs over the Mt. Adams district on June 24, 1947 — the same day as Kenneth Arnold's sighting (p. 4). The file relays an Air Transport Command summary on two crescent-shaped objects over Harmon Field, Guam on 14 August 1947 (p. 6), and the Fourth Air Force investigation of the Maury Island incident in which Harold Dahl and Fred Crisman claimed five discs dumped slag on a boat (p. 15–16). Investigators Lt. Frank Brown and Capt. William Davidson died when their B-25 crashed near Kelso, Washington on 1 August 1947 carrying material samples to Hamilton Field (p. 10–12). It also covers MIT analysis of metal fragments from West Rindge, New Hampshire (p. 26) and a Butte teletype on a 13 August 1947 Twin Falls, Idaho sighting by A. C. Urie and his two sons (p. 29).

## Key facts
- Johnson described round ~30-foot objects at ~1000 ft, silent, with a "big magenete"-like tail piece, banking into a cloud (p. 4).
- Three enlisted men of the 147th AACS Squadron saw two crescent-shaped objects over Guam at twice fighter speed, ~1,200 ft (p. 6).
- B-25 #1316 crashed ~13 miles south of Kelso at ~0236 PST after a left-engine fire; two of four bailed out (p. 10–11).
- T/Sgt. Mathews loaded a heavy cardboard carton into the rear compartment before the crash (p. 15).
- Maury Island claim (21 Jun 1947): five discs ~100 ft across with portholes at 500 ft; one bumped, dumping "tons" of material and killing Dahl's dog (p. 15–16).
- Agent noted weathered paint on Crisman's boat contradicted the alleged damage (p. 16).
- R. A. Palmer of Venture Press paid Arnold $200 to investigate; Dave Johnson urged the press be checked (p. 18–19).
- Recommendation to revoke Crisman's Air Reserve commission as "undesirable and unreliable" (p. 17).
- MIT analysis of West Rindge fragments: ordinary cast iron, heated, likely from a hollow 8"×3/16" cylinder; similar cylinders used on a New Mexico guided-missile project (p. 26).
- Urie and sons reported a 20×10×10-ft sky-blue object with side flames at ~1,000 mph down the Salmon River (p. 29).
