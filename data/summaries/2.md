## Summary
Section 2 of file 62-HQ-83894 (serials 53–100) compiles August 1947 field-office traffic on flying-disc reports. SAC San Francisco forwards Fourth Air Force reports from Lt. Col. Donald Springer and Maj. William R. Graham at Hamilton Field on Oregon and Arizona sightings (p. 2). Newark agents interview Charles Casella Jr. and Pvt. William Truex about a small round object over Hackensack, New Jersey on August 3, 1947 (pp. 4, 6–7). Detroit reports the Raymond Edward Lane "ball of white" and fused-sand incident at a Dow Chemical field in Midland, Michigan on July 9, with the residue partly radioactive and likened to Los Alamos sand (pp. 8, 10, 14). The War Department asks the Bureau to investigate Richard F. Shaver of Lily Lake, Illinois after an anonymous July 5 telegram tied him to the origin of flying saucers (pp. 17, 20). Seattle traffic covers the August 1, 1947 B-25 crash at Kelso, Washington that killed Capt. Davidson and Lt. Brown after their Maury Island interview of Harold Dahl and Fred Crisman, with Hoover directing a re-interview to confirm the hoax (pp. 24, 26, 28).

## Key facts
- Hackensack witnesses described a 30–40 inch dark or silver-blue object moving north about 200 yards above Summit Avenue, visible 15–60 seconds; Casella reportedly said "Maybe it's a flying saucer" (pp. 6–7).
- Dow Chemical analysis of Lane's sample found ordinary sand emitting ammonia gas, a near-pure silver nugget, fused sand, and a grayish component with extremely low radioactivity (pp. 8, 14).
- Raymond Lane (b. May 7, 1902, Freesoil, MI; 1924 burglary conviction) possessed radioactive luminous paint and was described as surly and antagonistic to plant protection officers (pp. 10, 14).
- An anonymous July 5, 1947 New York telegram to Maj. Paul Gaynor, AAF HQ, named Richard F. Shaver of Lily Lake, Illinois (pp. 17, 20).
- Maj. John D. Schindler Jr.'s July 7 report logged a saucer over Koshkonong–Elkhorn, Wisconsin at 6,000 mph and a second over Eagle–Muskego at 3,960 mph (p. 21).
- McChord Field intelligence attributed the Kelso B-25 crash to a burned-out exhaust stack, not sabotage; Davidson and Brown were carrying Maury Island disc fragments (p. 24).
- An 18-year-old Mrs. Little of 6327 Harper Avenue reported a disc over Lake Michigan off Jackson Park Beach at 3:30 a.m. on August 8, 1947 (p. 15).
