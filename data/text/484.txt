Seed Germination Protocol


1. Soak seeds in water for 30 minutes. This allows seeds to absorb water

   allowing for faster germination.

2. Place three sheets of paper in petri dishes and add enough water to

   moisten paper.

3. Place 10 seeds of each seed type onto paper to maintain adequate

   spacing. See Figure 2 for pattern of seed placement. Set up two petri

   dishes per seed type per location. Choose a location with high activity and

   an area of llow activity.

4. Place petri dishes on a table that is out of the flow of general traffic. See

   Figure 1 for setup.

5. Depending on humidity levels, add water as needed. Do not allow paper

   to dry out.

6. Try to maintain a consistent temperature, as close to 70 degrees as

   possible.

7. Check seeds for germination twice a day, once in the morning and once at

   night. See Figure 3 for pictures of actual seed germination.

8. Take close up photographs of seeds during examinations. Make sure

   seeds/seedlings are visible on photos. Use attached sheet for recording

   seed germination.

9. Run experiment for full deployment or until seedlings become too big for

   petri dishes, whichever occurs first.

   Additional measurements and observations:
   1. Note if any seedlings die off

   2. Bring germinated seeds back to BAASS following deployment.

Conclusion

      Many of the important BAASS projects, incl uding Northern Tier, entry into
classified archives, Project Oral History, as well as co ntact ing key people in the
military and private sector are on hold because BAASS cu rrently does not have
the Need t o Know for this information. BAAS$ in collaboration with t he sponsor
has recently taken t entative steps to contact some organizations within the USG
that may have access to material and archives. These contacts are at much too a
preliminary stage to assess the likelihood of the ir success or failure.

       In spite of th e above impediment s, significa nt progress has been made in
training and initially deploying BAASS field investigative teams during the fall 2009
months. A large part of the current investigations at BAASS are focused on the
Utah ranch an d surrounding areas. The BAASS MUFON relatio nship is being
closely monitored especially since the case numbers t hat MUFON are alerting
BAASS with, although improving, are still lower t han originally expected. The
BAASS database of cases is now larger than any other database owned by UAP
organizations in North America. It should be noted that BAASS has initiated a
separate Capella database for t he events on the Utah ranch and surrounding
areas, a report on which will be forthcoming in the coming mo nths.
